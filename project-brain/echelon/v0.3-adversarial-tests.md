# echelon — v0.3 Adversarial Tests

## Purpose

v0.1 established a narrow debugging wedge.

v0.2 made that wedge harder and showed a synthetic win over `fixed_heuristic`.

v0.3 exists to attack that result directly.

The goal is not to extend the product surface.
The goal is to falsify a fragile win if it is only benchmark-shaped.

## Core question

Can the debugging-core result survive adversarial evaluation without collapsing into:

- label leakage
- static ordering bias
- decorative transitions
- synthetic cases that flatter our router by construction

## Scope

v0.3 stays narrow:

- debugging only
- same small regime set
- no new product surface
- no UI
- no new ontology

## Test set

### 1. Label permutation

Hypothesis:

- routed policy performance should be largely invariant to family naming and action ordering

Fail if:

- the routed win disappears after permutation

### 2. Regime ablation

Hypothesis:

- runtime transitions materially improve search behavior

Method:

- disable transitions after the initial regime selection

Fail if:

- full routed and ablated routed perform nearly identically

### 3. Misleading-evidence reversal

Hypothesis:

- routed policy should recover from strong early wrong evidence faster than `fixed_heuristic`

Method:

- first two observations reinforce the wrong family
- later evidence flips the answer

Fail if:

- routed persists on the wrong family as long as or longer than `fixed_heuristic`

## Metrics

Primary:

- repeated failed-path suppression
- cost before decisive root-cause signal
- success under budget

Secondary:

- transition count
- hysteresis
- dead-end persistence

## Decision rule

v0.3 passes if:

- the current synthetic win survives all three adversarial tests

v0.3 fails if:

- permutation breaks the result
- transitions turn out to be decorative
- misleading evidence traps routed policy as badly as the fixed heuristic

## Interpretation

Passing v0.3 does not prove generality.

It does mean:

- the focused debugging result is less likely to be a false positive from obvious synthetic bias

That is the right next standard before adding wider benchmarks or real replay cases.
