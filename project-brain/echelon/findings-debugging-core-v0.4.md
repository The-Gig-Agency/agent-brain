- expanded permutation checks

- one stronger baseline
- generated hidden holdout cases

## What changed from v0.3

`v0.3` showed that the current result survived three adversarial tests.

`v0.4` adds four stronger constraints:

- simple renaming and action reordering still do not collapse the result
- routed policy does not lose when simple narrowing is the right move
- routed policy beats one stronger non-trivial baseline, not just weak baselines
- routed policy retains an advantage on generated holdout cases from unseen seeds

## Why this matters

This is the first pass where the debugging wedge looks meaningfully more robust against the main synthetic false-positive risks:

- surface-pattern leakage
- over-rewarding broad exploration
- weak baseline selection
- overfitting to a hand-authored case list

That does not prove generality.
But it does make the focused claim more serious.

## What this still does not prove

This is still synthetic evidence.

It does **not** yet prove:

- transfer to real bug histories or incident traces
- robustness against stronger model-based agents
- broad debugging superiority outside this experimental setup

The generated holdout is stronger than hand-authored development cases, but it is still generated by our simulator.

## Best interpretation

The right claim is:

- the routed debugging-core wedge now survives a substantially harder synthetic hardening pass

The wrong claim would be:

- adaptive search-policy control is now broadly proven for debugging

## Best next move

The next honest step is not wider product scope.

It is:

- tiny real-world replay traces
- one or two stronger execution-grounded baselines
- continued blind evaluation discipline

That is the right path if we want to keep the claim credible.
